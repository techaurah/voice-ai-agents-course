# Solution, Lesson 22

At a threshold of three digit words, the count is still 13 digit strings, identical to the threshold-four count. The lower threshold finds nothing new in this corpus, because the corpus contains no three-digit spoken runs: times are spoken in at most two digit words in these fixtures ("nine fifteen" style fragments are part of longer runs or fail to parse as digit strings).

The value of the corpus test is precisely that this could not be known in advance. Reasoned about abstractly, a threshold of three "should" catch more; measured against the actual stored text, it catches nothing extra here, and on a different corpus (one with spoken times like "one two thirty" or partial numbers) it might. Redaction rules are hypotheses about data; the corpus is where hypotheses go to be counted.
